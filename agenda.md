      <td>10:00 - 11:00</td>
      <td>Sharing less, learning more: synthetic data and federated learning as approaches to sensitive data</td>
      <td>Open meeting; joining details to be confirmed</td>
      <td>
        <p>Sharing research data contributes to transparency, reproducibility and scientific discovery, but sensitive data containing personal information cannot always be shared openly. How can researchers collaborate and generate knowledge while protecting privacy? This webinar explores synthetic data, which uses statistical or AI-based methods to generate artificial data that mimic real datasets, and federated learning, which trains machine-learning models across distributed datasets without moving or sharing the underlying data. Join us to explore the opportunities, challenges and practical realities of these privacy-preserving technologies.</p>
        <p><strong>Format:</strong> Webinar<br>
        <strong>Audience:</strong> Researchers and research support staff.<br>
        <strong>Prior knowledge:</strong> None required.</p>
      </td>
      <td><strong>Speakers:</strong> Qinyi Liu (UiB) and a speaker from NTNU to be confirmed. Qinyi Liu is an Assistant Professor at the City University of Macau specializing in trustworthy AI and educational data; her research examines synthetic data and privacy-enhancing technologies.<br>
        <strong>Co-organisers:</strong> Emma (NTNU), Nivedita (UiB)</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>12:00 - 13:00</td>
      <td>Anonymizing research data with Amnesia</td>
      <td><a href="https://dtudk.zoom.us/j/61471652223">Zoom meeting</a></td>
      <td>
        <p>GDPR strictly limits how personal data can be used and shared, while properly anonymized data fall outside its scope and can be shared without consent. This webinar explains what anonymization is, why pseudonymization is not enough, and how guarantees such as k-anonymity and k^m-anonymity protect individuals against re-identification. We then demonstrate <a href="https://amnesia.openaire.eu/">Amnesia</a>, the free, open-source anonymization tool offered through OpenAIRE, on an example dataset, and discuss its limitations and how it compares with synthetic data.</p>
        <p><strong>Format:</strong> Online presentation with a live demo and Q&amp;A<br>
        <strong>Audience:</strong> Researchers, PhD students, data stewards and research data management staff working with personal or sensitive data, such as health, social science or survey data.<br>
        <strong>Prior knowledge:</strong> None required. Basic familiarity with tabular data such as spreadsheets or CSV files is helpful; no programming or prior anonymization knowledge is needed.</p>
      </td>
      <td><strong>Speaker:</strong> Dr. Manolis Terrovitis, Research Director at the Information Management Systems Institute (IMSI) of the Athena Research Center and its Data Protection Officer. He specializes in privacy-preserving data publishing, data anonymization and big data management, and leads the development of Amnesia.<br>
        <strong>Organiser:</strong> Jitka Stilund Hansen, DTU</td>
    </tr>

      <td>Handle with extra care - Lessons learned from building a research infrastructure for abuse prevention data</td>
      <td><a href="https://ki-se.zoom.us/j/68874023367">Zoom meeting</a></td>
      <td>
        <p>The DARE repository collects research data on child sexual abuse prevention to facilitate future research and the development of abuse prevention strategies. Initially, data is submitted by members of the consortia Building a Global Data Infrastructure for Child Sexual Abuse Prevention Research: the DARE PREVENTION Repository and PRISM Cohort Study. In the long term, the repository will welcome contributions from any researcher or institution holding sexual abuse prevention data.</p>
        <p>All datasets in the collection are described with rich metadata. However, the ethical and legal circumstances around collecting abuse prevention data create different restrictions on transferring, accessing and reusing datasets. This webinar explains these constraints and discusses workarounds and solutions for creating a foundation for open, collaborative and reproducible research in psychiatry and child sexual abuse prevention.</p>
        <p><strong>Format:</strong> Lunch webinar<br>
